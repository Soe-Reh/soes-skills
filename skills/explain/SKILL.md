# Explain

Explain the idea so the reader gets it on the first read, without making it less accurate.

## 1. Know the reader

Decide who reads it: a beginner, a PM, an engineer from another field, a recruiter. If unclear, assume a smart person who is new to the topic.

## 2. Write it

1. Start with what the thing does and why it matters, in one or two sentences.
2. One idea per sentence. Short sentences.
3. Use common words. Keep a technical term only when it is the right term, and define it the first time.
4. Spell out an acronym the first time.
5. Say who does what: "the server checks the token", not "the token is checked".
6. Put steps in the order they happen.
7. Give one concrete example when the idea is abstract.
8. Keep what is known, what is assumed, and what you recommend in separate lines.

Use a list for steps or parallel items. Use prose when the ideas depend on each other.

## 3. Check it

Before sending, read it as the reader:

- Every "it" and "this" points to one clear thing.
- Every term is defined or already familiar to this reader.
- Each step says what changes and why.
- Nothing was cut that makes it wrong. If something is uncertain, say what would confirm it.

Remove filler, hype words, and jargon that only sounds smart.
